snpz — team brief for William and Aiden (written Sunday night, Sept 6)

What we're selling this week

An AI phone receptionist for barbershops. It picks up when nobody at the shop does, answers

questions, and books the cut. Booksy/Vagaro-style booking software is the bigger product, but

until business texting is approved (carrier registration, a couple of weeks) the pitch is the

receptionist only. Price: $27.99/month for one chair, $20 for each extra chair, 60

receptionist minutes per chair. No contract, no free trial, no money-back. Never negotiate on

the phone; the offer page holds each shop's price for 30 days.

Who we call, in order

  • Phone-only shops (about 1,900 in PA). No booking software at all. Nothing to integrate:

    • the receptionist books on the calendar that comes with it. Easiest yes.

  • Square shops (about 200). Square has a public API, so the receptionist can book straight

    • into their Square calendar.

  • Booksy shops (about 1,700). No API. For them it's answer, quote prices/hours, take a

    • message. Weakest pitch until texting is live, so last.

    Squire, theCut, Vagaro, GlossGenius, Fresha: no usable API. Not on the list this week.

    About 1 in 6 "phone-only" shops is secretly on Booksy — so always ask.

Monday is Labor Day. The list puts shops whose Google listing says "open Monday" first, then

the ones showing regular hours, then the rest. Closed ones sink to the bottom.

The script (this is the whole thing)

  • "Hey, is this [owner], the owner?" — not in? Owner not in, call back (pick the time).
  • "How many calls do you think you miss a week?"
  • "How many chairs do you have?" (skip if the card already knows)
  • "With three chairs you're probably missing about three calls a week — about one

    • appointment. That's about $120 by the end of the month." *(the panel does this math from

    his chairs and his cut price; it's an estimate, say "probably", never "on average shops…")*

  • "I built you an AI receptionist. I'm switching you over — ask it the hardest, most

    • confusing question you can, it'll try to book you at your own shop." → **Put [owner] on the

    AI, listen, then Bring him back**.

  • "When should I call you back to get this set up for you?" → Wants it, set up (pick the

    • time). Then send him the Google Meet link with Text from my phone.

Outcomes are only these: No answer (goes back in the list) · Owner not in, call back ·

Wants it, set up · Not interested (off the list). We never leave voicemails.

The tools

  • snpz.ai/sales → sign in with your Gmail address (Grady has your password). Press

    • Ctrl+Shift+R the first time. You land on the calling page: next-up list top left

    (shop, owner, chairs, software), leaderboard top right (answered calls, call-to-meeting %),

    five buttons in the centre — **CALL / HANG UP · OWNER AWAY (set the time) · SWITCH TO AI /

    SWITCH BACK · PHASE TWO (set the time) · NOT INTERESTED** — scheduled calls bottom left,

    the script as chat bubbles bottom right. Keys: c, 1, a, 2, x. Hang up before anyone

    answers = missed call, back in the list under "Missed calls".

  • Phase two = the owner wants a second call after work to learn more. That is the meeting

    • the leaderboard counts. Only answered calls count as calls.

  • Grady's full admin screen is still at snpz.ai/sales → "Sales"; the simple addresses are

    • snpz.ai/sales, /calendar, /call, /start, /signup.

  • Talk to each other without walking over: "show this to William" puts a page, picture or

    • video in front of everything on his main monitor (both ways, Aiden too). Your Claude does

    it: hub\screen\show.ps1 grady <https url> or scripts\show-file.ps1 grady <file>.

    Your Claude can also ask Grady's Claude anything about snpz on the inbox line (the repo's

    CLAUDE.md explains; it sets both lines up by itself the first time you open the repo).

  • Each of us calls from our own number. Grady ends in 5081, William 7167, Aiden 7245. All

    • three passed the spam check tonight.

  • Next / Call claims the shop to you. Nobody else can dial it. If you both click at the

    • same instant, the server hands out two different shops, guaranteed. Callbacks are open to

    whoever is free when the time comes — the card carries everything we've learned.

  • Notes box: hold Right Alt and talk after the call ("John, four chairs, seven years, call

    • back Thursday at five"). It fills the profile.

  • Links on the card: website, Facebook, Google listing — use them to find the owner's name

    • before you dial when the card says "ask for the owner".

    • one fixed link; screen-share the calendar and set the shop up while they watch.

  • Music: Grady's PC pauses the room music the moment any of our calls is answered.

Rules

  • The demo receptionist says it's a demo in its first sentence. Never let a shop think it's

    • live.

  • Never record a call. Pennsylvania needs everyone's consent; our notes are what we heard.
  • No made-up statistics. The money line is *their* numbers plus a rule of thumb, said as

    • "probably".

  • Don't say "I built one off your menu" — most shops have no menu on file.
  • Business lines are outside the Do Not Call list. If a number is clearly someone's personal

    • cell, skip it.
