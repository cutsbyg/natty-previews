# WSL Incorporated — what's missing, and what to ask them for

Meeting agenda, not a defect list. Every line is either something the page carries honestly
at half strength, or something only they can give us.

## Ask them for these — in priority order

1. **Photographs of the outside of finished houses.** The real gap. Their library is strong
   inside and thin outside: dozens of good interiors, very few exteriors that aren't under
   construction, on bare dirt or flat-grey overcast. The hero is their best exterior and it
   still had to be widened by outpainting. It is also the reason for the one measured miss below (panel-vs-hardscape). One afternoon with a drone at
   three finished houses fixes their whole site permanently.
2. **A portrait of Josh, shot portrait-orientation, from about eight feet.** There is exactly
   one usable public photo of him — standing at the road sign, in a ball cap, full length,
   taken from perhaps thirty feet. §1a step 12 frames every founder the same way, top of head
   to hip, so it is cropped there (`img/josh-cut.png`, 741×1392, cut at 55% of his standing
   height, sign excluded). That is the correct framing and it matches the other site's founder
   almost exactly — both render a head of roughly 68–70px. It is simply a distant photograph: he
   is soft at that magnification because the pixels were never there. A portrait shot close
   removes the upscaling, not the crop.
3. **A second photograph of Josh, in context.** §3 wants the founder band to use a *different*
   photograph of the same person. Only one exists, so that band uses the office group photo —
   a different photograph doing a different job, but not him on a site.
4. **A line from Josh in his own words.** Nothing first-person from him exists in any source,
   so the founder band carries a customer's words about him instead.
5. **Photographs of the three credentials.** The proof band ships as three typeset documents —
   CAPS, VA-adapted housing, HIC# PA011481 — because no photograph of a physical certificate
   exists anywhere. §5 wants the artifact photographed. Ten minutes with a phone and the
   framed certificates on the office wall upgrades that band, and the layout takes photographs
   without reflowing.
6. **Individual headshots of the office team.** Nine people are named with their roles because
   they published that themselves. No individual portraits exist, so the strip is a name grid
   rather than faces.
7. **An email address.** Their contact page obfuscates it, so the CTA links to their form
   instead of a `mailto:`.
8. **Which of these photographs are the same house — and one exterior of each.** This is now the
   top photo ask after #1, and it is worth money in the meeting. Their library arrives as loose
   interiors with no job attached, and the pairing we were handed turned out to be wrong seven
   times in eight (audit below). Sixteen good photographs are currently doing the work of eight,
   because we can only prove the room, never the house. *"Tell us which of these are the same
   house, and send one exterior of each"* turns eight rooms into eight identifiable projects
   with an outside and an inside — and it is a ten-minute answer for them.
9. **Which house is which by name.** Their captions don't identify the job, so work cards are
   described by room and carry no town. Guessing would be a fabrication.

## Measured, and written down rather than hidden

**§1a step 0 — the hero's landmarks, read off a ruler overlay once, % of frame height:**
house ridge 8% · roof-overhang tip at **x 46.5%** · concrete retaining wall **49%** ·
rock-garden dry-stone wall 63% · driveway 88%.

- **§1a.0a, the composition contract, is a REPORT on this frame, not a gate (§1a.0a′).**
  `heroplace.py` returns *subject spans x 0–100%* — the frame is wall-to-wall forest, so the
  detector cannot isolate a subject and the ≥45% subject-left-edge and ≥40% clear-margin rows
  are unreachable as stated. Hand-measured instead: the house's leftmost built pixel is the
  roof-overhang tip at **x 46.5%**, and the panel's right edge is set to **42.0%** — exactly
  the 4% gap §1a.0a asks for. This is the single strongest argument for ask #1.
- **The panel's bottom-right corner overlaps the concrete retaining wall.** Hand-measured, the
  first built thing under the panel's x-span is that wall at **49%** of frame height.
  `panelcheck`'s derived limit is **73.2%** — the wall's contiguous run inside the panel's
  x-span is 3–4 cells against the tool's 6-cell threshold, so the detector never sees it. The
  rail finishes at 58.5–61.2%, which clears the *derived* limit by 12.0–14.7 points and passes,
  but sits over the left ~60% of that wall. It is not fixable within the geometry: clearing 49%
  by §1a step 8c's 3% would put the panel's bottom at 46% of the frame, which cannot hold the
  five elements §1a.11a requires at any legible size, and would also fail the reference's
  measured `y1`. The hand-measured limit and the derived one are both recorded here per §1a's
  override rule. The fix is a wider exterior photograph, not a smaller panel.
- **§1a step 2 fill ratio:** the headline fills **84%** of its words column at 1200 and 1440,
  and **74%** at 1920 where the 4.4rem cap takes over. Below the 85% target at every width;
  the built-work limit outranks the fill ratio and the levers above it (panel padding, the
  support line's length, and the panel's width up to §1a.0a's gap) are all spent.
- **§3.2a rule 1 contrast:** the sampled brand `#ED1C24` measures **4.0:1** on paper — under
  4.5:1. The role splits as the rule directs: `--brand` is a ground with white type on it,
  `--brand-deep` carries brand-coloured type on paper. Worth telling them, because it
  affects their signage too.
- **2026-08-15 — the red ramp was taken down a stop.** Grady: *"the red is way too bright —
  trying to avoid using really bright, terrible colors that are hard to look at."* Same hue
  (all four tokens sit at 358°, the logo's hue); saturation and lightness only.
  `--brand #a51419 → #77181b` (358/78/36 → 358/66/28), `--brand-deep #750e12 → #570f12`,
  `--brand-lift #ed1c24 → #b42d31`. Measured after: white on `--brand` **10.88:1** (was
  7.74), `--brand-deep` on paper **13.04:1** (was 10.58), on a white card **14.17:1**.
  **Worth saying in the meeting:** the site no longer shows their exact signage red at full
  strength anywhere large. If they want it back, that is a conversation, not a silent change.
- **§3.4 phone height:** the page *without* the intake form is 7.86 viewports at 375×812

## Before this goes live

**Hosting is not set up.** The site is a folder on a disk.

1. Pick a host and a deploy command. Static files, so almost anything works.
2. Point the domain at it, or stand it up on a subdomain.
3. **Remove `<meta name="robots" content="noindex,nofollow">`** from `index.html`. It is there
   deliberately — a spec site indexing under the client's name is a real problem — and it must
   come off the moment they sign, or none of the SEO work counts.
4. Submit a sitemap in Google Search Console and verify the property.
5. **Point the domain at the analytics server and register the site.** The same process serves the
   pages and the owner's dashboard, so there is no second host and no CORS:

   ```
   node analytics/server.js --add-site <slug> "<Business name>" <domain> <path/to/build>
   ```

   It prints a generated password once. Give it to the owner; it is the login behind the *Owner
   login* link already in the footer. `--passwd <slug>` rotates it later.

Steps 3 and 4 are the two that get forgotten. A live site still carrying `noindex` looks like
it is working and is invisible to Google. Step 5 fails the same quiet way: the pages serve
perfectly from anywhere, and if they are served from anywhere else `/a.js` and `/admin` are just
404s, so the site counts nothing and the owner's link goes nowhere.
