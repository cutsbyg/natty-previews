## 🛑 0 — ASK BEN BEFORE THIS SITE GOES ANYWHERE

**B&D is an Amish family business, and this build puts a photograph of the family on the
page.** The photograph is `Ben Stoltzfus and the team`, published by B&D themselves on
their own About page — so using it is defensible. It is still not the same thing as their
permission for *us* to put it on a site we are selling them.

Some Plain families object to being photographed at all, and a family that tolerates one
photo on a page nobody visits may feel differently about it sitting large on a homepage.
**Ask, in person, before this is shown to anyone outside the room:**

1. "You have this photo on your About page — are you happy for it to be on the new site?"
2. "Is there anyone in it who would rather not be shown?"
3. "Would you rather we used no photographs of people at all?" — if the answer is yes,
   the founder band swaps to the §3b silhouette treatment already used in the hero and
   nothing else on the page changes.

Do not remove it pre-emptively either. It is the single most persuasive thing on the page
after the hero, and they published it themselves.

`wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Ben-Stoltzfus-and-the-team.jpg` · figcaption and alt text both

## 🛑 1 — THE ASKS, in the order they are worth money

### 1.1 A photograph of Ben on his own — **the top ask**
The hero panel currently carries a **plain blue silhouette** where the owner's cut-out goes.
That is deliberate and it is honest: a flat single-colour shape reads as *photo missing*,
which is exactly true. It is not a stock photo and it is not a generated face — those are
never shipped.

**Why there is no photograph.** The team photo is a group of eleven, standing in a row
indoors, and **nothing on the page says which man is Ben.** No "left to right", no names,
no positional cue. Two men in it are old enough to be the principal. Guessing which one is
Ben and cutting him out is the one mistake that cannot be walked back in a meeting, so it
was not made.

**What to get:** one photograph of Ben, standing, from the top of his head to below his
hands — outdoors on a job is ideal, phone camera is fine, overcast light is fine. It drops
straight into the same box at the same geometry; nothing else on the page moves.
Second-best: someone points at the group photo and says which one he is.

### 1.2 Photographs of the credentials — **the whole proof band is missing**
The blueprint's proof band (`credential → the work that earned it → where it was published`)
**was deleted from this build**, because B&D publishes no award, no press, no association
membership and no certification anywhere we could find, and a band of service descriptions
with stock photos is worse than no band at all.

The band comes back the moment any of these exists as a **photograph of the physical object**:
- The **PA Home Improvement Contractor registration certificate** — they publish
  `PA lic # 114340` in their own footer, but the paper itself is not photographed anywhere.
- The **certificate of insurance**.
- Any manufacturer certification, trade-association membership, or local-paper mention.

Photographed on a workbench in daylight. Three of them and the band is back at full strength.

### 1.3 Six job names, and which photographs belong to each
B&D publishes exactly **three** project names — *Stevenson Deck Project*, *Carlisle Second
Story Addition*, *Mimari Basement Finish* — and **no** locations, dates or descriptions on
any of them. Two of those three names are a past customer's surname, which is not ours to
republish, so five of the six project cards carry a plain description instead
(`The master bathroom`, `A finished basement`, …).

**Ask:** name six jobs the way you'd say them out loud, give each a town and a year, and
say which photographs belong to which. That is the single largest quality gain available on
this page and it costs one conversation.

### 1.4 Google Business Profile — somebody has to open it with their eyes
The rating on the page (**5.0**, and **"Over 12 reviews"** in the reviews band) was read off
**B&D's own website**, from the Smash Balloon widget that syncs their Google profile — *not*
off the Google panel itself. The newest review the widget shows is a year old, so the cached
figures may be stale.

**Before launch:** open the Google Business Profile
(Place ID `ChIJcVIKEYIvyYkRma83n6T4rU8`), read the rating and the count, and correct the two
places they appear (hero badge, reviews band count line). While you are there, check for a
**duplicate Google listing** — two profiles split the reviews and the ranking, and merging
them is free.

### 1.5 The email address
`office@bdbuildersandcontractors.com` is on the page (CTA button and footer). **It does not
appear anywhere on their website** — every page was grepped for `mailto:` and for any
`@`-address; zero hits. Confirm it is current and monitored, or replace it.

### 1.6 Business hours
Not published anywhere — not on the site, not in the Facebook public metadata. The footer
office column therefore shows the address only. One line from Ben fills it.

### 1.7 The slogan on the page — is it the right one?
The headline is **"Your Home, Your Way."**, verbatim from their own home page. But it is
*body copy* — an H2 in the middle of the page — not a slogan on the logo, the sign or the
trucks. The blueprint ranks signage and vehicle lettering **above** website copy, and a site
crawl cannot read a truck door.

**Ask:** "Is there a line on your sign or your trucks? Because we'd rather use that."
Runner-up already on the site, if they want it instead:
**"An Honest, Dedicated, Reliable Builder"** (home page sub-heading), or
**"Honest, dependable and very efficient, start to finish…we do it all."** (About page —
currently used as the founder band's pull-quote).

Also worth raising: **their own logo and their WordPress site name disagree with the
business name.** The logo lockup says *B&D Builders and Contractors LLC*, but the site's
internal name and every project page title say *B&D Restoration*, and the Facebook page's
banner artwork says *B&D RESTORATION* too. Which is current?

### 1.8 Photographs that would raise the page immediately
- **Interiors of the black barn house.** It is the most striking building in their whole
  library and both photographs of it are exteriors (see divergence D4).
- **Individual portraits of the crew.** There are none anywhere, so the crew strip
  (4–6 named faces, the most-praised element on these pages) **does not exist on this
  build**. Twenty minutes against one wall on one overcast day fixes it permanently.
- **A dusk exterior of a finished new home**, shot wide, with room around the building.
  See divergence D1 — the hero photograph is beautiful and *too tight*, and one 30-minute
  shoot would remove three separate compromises at once.

## 4 — DIVERGENCES FROM THE BLUEPRINT, each with its number

Every one of these is a measurement that was run, failed, and is reported rather than
quietly worked around.

### D1 — The hero cannot carry a panel on the photograph. It is beside it instead.
The hero was fixed upstream (Grady chose `p0157`), so the composition contract is a report,
not a gate. Measured with `_tools/heroplace.py` on the **served** file:

| §1a.0a wants | `hero-wide.jpg` measures |
|---|---|
| subject's left edge ≥ 45% of frame width | **0.0%** |
| clear left margin ≥ 40% of frame width | **0.0%** — the house spans 0–100% |
| ≥8% headroom above the ridge (§1b) | **2.1%** |
| ≥6% below the base (§1b) | **8.9%** ✓ |
| a free rectangle the headline can use, on the **left** | the only rectangle returned is **17.5% wide, on the right** (5/5 grids agree: bottom-right) |

A panel placed anywhere inside that frame lands on the client's own building, which §7 rule
0 forbids outright. §1a step 10 covers exactly this case — *"a rectangle that exists but
cannot hold the headline counts as no rectangle, and the slab below the photo is then
correct rather than forbidden"* — so the build ships the sanctioned **split hero**: the
photograph whole and uncropped in its own framed box, and the glass panel beside it on the
left, carrying all four first-screen facts. `panelcheck.py` at 1200 / 1440 / 1920 reports
*"panel is below the photo — nothing to clip"*, which is the pass this layout can earn.

**The fix is a photograph, not CSS.** One wide dusk exterior with sky above the ridge and
lawn to one side, and the hero becomes full-bleed with the panel on the photograph.

### D2 — One hero composition, served at three sizes, not three composed crops
§1b asks for three separately composed frames (wide / mid / phone). p0157's subject spans
0–100% of the width and 2.1%–91.1% of the height: **any recomposition cuts the building**,
which is a §1b disqualifier. All three breakpoints therefore serve the same composition at
1888 / 1400 / 900px. The media box's `aspect-ratio` equals the served asset's exactly
(1888:1366), so there are no letterbox bars at any width.

### D3 — The proof band was deleted
No award, no press, no certification, no association membership exists. §1.5's substitution
ladder needs an artifact **photographed as an object**; none can be had today. §1.5 is
explicit that filler in the proof position is worse than absence, so the band is gone and
the alternation was re-checked: `paper → brand → paper → brand → paper → brand`, strictly
alternating with no two grounds touching. See ask 1.2 to bring it back.

### D4 — The black barn house ships with two exterior photographs
§2.3 wants an outside and an inside per project. B&D's library contains **no interior of the
black barn** — the two frames are the entry elevation and the long elevation with the
workshop doors, which do show different parts of the building but are both exteriors. It was
shipped rather than cut because it is the most impressive structure they have. Ask for
interiors (1.8) and it becomes a proper pair.

### D5 — No crew strip
§3a wants 4–6 named faces. There is **not one individual portrait of any crew member**
anywhere on the website or in 230 Facebook photographs. Per the contract the strip deletes
rather than shipping placeholder tiles. The team photograph carries the band instead.
See ask 1.8.

Related, and worth stating plainly: §5 asks that three kinds of photograph all reach the
page — **the wide shot of finished work** (yes, the hero), **people doing the work** (no —
the team photo is a line-up indoors, not a crew on a site), and **the owner** (no — see D6).
Two of the three are gaps, and both are fixed by one afternoon with a phone camera.

### D6 — The owner is a silhouette, not a photograph
§1's first screen carries *this house · this man · these words · this score*. Three of the
four are real. The fourth is §3b's standin, at the exact geometry the real cut-out will
occupy, so the photograph is a one-file swap. See ask 1.1.

### D7 — Project names
Five of six cards carry a plain description instead of a name, because the only names B&D
publishes are two customer surnames and one town. §2.10 forbids inventing a surname, a town
or a year to fill the slot. See ask 1.3.

### D8 — Small measured overruns, listed for completeness
- Nav bar **70px** against a 50–66px budget — §10's type minimums (logo ≥52px, links
  ≥1.02rem, phone ≥1.15rem) need the height, and §10 says not to compress type to keep a
  thin bar.
- Work band **1183px** against a ≈1040px target (+14%, inside the ±15% band) and well inside
  the 1.5-viewport hard limit.
- Stats strip **69–74px** against ≤70px, at 1920 only.
- Whole page **4.4 viewports** at 1440×900 and **7.0** at 375×812, against bars of 6 and 8.
  Shorter than the ≈5.4 reference because the proof band and the crew strip are absent.
